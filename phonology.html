Phonology

Phonology concerns sound patterns in a language. Phonemic awareness is awareness of individual speech sounds; phonics connects these sounds to written graphemes. Neither is the same as morphology, the study of meaningful word parts.

Useful concepts

Phoneme
A contrastive speech sound, such as the first sound in pat compared with bat.
Primary stress
The most prominent syllable in a word. For example, the noun REcord contrasts with the verb reCORD.
Schwa
An unstressed vowel sound /ə/, as at the beginning of about. Its spelling may vary.
Consonant blends
Adjacent consonants that retain separate sounds, such as /s/ and /t/ in stop.
Suffix pronunciation
Plural -s can sound like /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/: cats, dogs, buses. Past -ed can sound like /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/: walked, played, wanted.

For Australian English pronunciation, account for dialect differences and consult a suitable pronunciation dictionary. Do not invent IPA or infer speech sounds from letters alone.
