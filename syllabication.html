Syllabication

A syllable is a spoken unit organised around a vowel sound or syllabic consonant. Written syllable-division conventions may differ from the spoken division and from morpheme boundaries.

Common instructional syllable types

TypeTypical patternExample
ClosedEnds in a consonant, often short vowelcat
OpenEnds in a vowel, often long vowelgo
Vowel-consonant-eFinal e often marks long vowelcake
Vowel teamMultiple letters represent a vowel soundrain
Vowel-rVowel spelling followed by rcar
Consonant-leFinal consonant plus letable

These are teaching categories, not exceptionless rules; in Australian English, postvocalic r is typically not pronounced before a consonant or pause.

Worked examples

unhelpful: un–help–ful (three syllables); morphology un- + help + -ful.

rebuild: re–build (two syllables); morphology re- + build.

Remember that stress is not necessarily on a base and that the number of written vowel letters does not determine the number of syllables.
