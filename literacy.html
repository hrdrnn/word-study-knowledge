Literacy

Word study can connect meaning, spelling, pronunciation and grammatical use. Teach the relationship explicitly, using examples that fit students’ subject and year level.

Map to Meaning

Identify meaningful prefixes, bases and suffixes where present. Give a one-to-three-word meaning for each and an accurate definition of no more than eight words. Avoid forcing an affix into a word.

Seven-section classroom routine

  1. Map to Meaning.
  2. Part of Speech, including relevant morphological clues.
  3. Pronunciation: syllables, primary stress and relevant sound–spelling patterns.
  4. Synonyms appropriate for the chosen meaning.
  5. Antonyms or clearly labelled contextual opposites.
  6. One to five contextual sentences of eight to eighteen words each, including the target word and genuine related forms.
  7. Up to ten additional words sharing a meaningful component.

For Years 7–12, adjust vocabulary and examples to the learning area. Use explicit modelling, guided practice, cumulative review and opportunities to apply new vocabulary in authentic reading and writing. Cite only consulted sources and acknowledge uncertainty.
